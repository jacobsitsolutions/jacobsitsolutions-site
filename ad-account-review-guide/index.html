01 / Define

Decide what the review covers.

Write down the domain, account population, selected groups, collection time, purpose, reviewer and due date. A user/group-membership review does not automatically cover every permission, application or system an account can reach.

Agree on the evidence and decision process with the people responsible for the review. Keep this scope beside the eventual report so its boundaries remain visible.

02 / Inspect

Check the evidence before the flags.

Use an authorized collection method. Check collection errors, unreadable properties, unresolved references and what the method excludes before treating the output as a complete starting list.

For each important row, consider account state, account age, recorded activity and relevant group relationships. A selected administrative group is only part of the privilege picture. Group membership alone does not calculate every effective permission.

An older logon timestamp is a question to investigate.

Replicated timestamps can lag, and a missing value is not proof of never logging on. Service and emergency accounts need their owner's explanation. Do not turn an indicator into an automatic removal decision.

03 / Decide

Give each decision a person and a reason.

Use the blank worksheet for a scoped review. Fill it with authorized evidence held within your organization. The blank file contains no accounts or suggested decisions.

  • Owner: who can explain why the account or relationship is needed.
  • Reviewer: who actually evaluated the row.
  • Decision and reason: what should happen and the context supporting it.
  • Date and reference: when the decision was recorded and where approval or follow-up is tracked.

If a decision cannot be made, record the question and route it to the responsible person. A completed checkbox without the underlying review does not establish that access was appropriate.

04 / Follow through

Keep a decision separate from a completed change.

A worksheet saying "remove" is not evidence that removal happened. Make approved changes through your normal administrative process, retain the approval and change reference, and verify the result afterward.

Our blank worksheet includes separate action status, completion date and verification-reference fields for this follow-up. Those extra tracking fields are a manual template aid; the pilot's generated worksheet does not automate change tracking.

05 / Retain

Preserve the starting evidence and the review record.

Keep the original collected package intact and edit a separate working copy. Retain the final decisions, approvals, change records and any verification evidence under your organization's rules.

Protect directory information with appropriate access controls. A file hash can help compare a file to a preserved reference; it does not authenticate an unsigned manifest or prove that the collection was accurate.

A current snapshot also cannot prove what access existed during an earlier review. Record gaps honestly rather than reconstructing missing history as if it were observed.

Use the templates

Make the next review easier to start.

The checklist is a blank preparation and follow-up record. The worksheet has headers only; enter one review item per row and keep supporting evidence separately. Use your own decision vocabulary and required approvals.
